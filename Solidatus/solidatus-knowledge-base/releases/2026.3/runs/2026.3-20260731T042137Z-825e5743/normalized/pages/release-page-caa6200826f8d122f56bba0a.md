# Understand Solidatus models

A common definition of a model is “a representation of some aspect of the world that is based on simplifying assumptions.” This definition fits Solidatus models in some respects but not others.

Like all models, Solidatus models are representations “of” something, usually a complex technical infrastructure landscape in which data is stored, processed, analysed, and fed into reporting tools.

Unlike other models, Solidatus models are designed to ingest and store the full complexity of what they represent without simplification. Once this complexity is store, models tools that make it possible to dissect and simplify the complexity of what they represent.

Given their flexible structure, Solidatus models can represent anything from rail networks to supply chains to the application of regulations to data systems and pipelines.

In all of these cases, models are tools that make it possible to capture critical information and easily answer urgent questions about complex technical systems and processes.
